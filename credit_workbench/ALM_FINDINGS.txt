--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. THE POINT-IN-TIME CORRECTION THAT CHANGED THE DESIGN
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
NSE's annual-report index carries a dissemination timestamp. Where
`broadcast_dttm` is "-", the filename embeds it (AR_15799_DHFL_2018_2019_
05092019164529 -> 05-09-2019 16:45:29), so a publication date is recoverable
for every report.

Checking those timestamps against the event dates removed reports that week
one would have used:

  DHFL          FY2018-19 report published 2019-09-05  -> AFTER the
                4 June 2019 default. Unusable.
  IL&FS Trans   FY2017-18 report published 2018-09-24  -> AFTER the
                4 Sept 2018 group CP default. Unusable.
  Rel Home Fin  FY2018-19 report published 2019-09-07  -> AFTER the
                1 Sept 2019 default. Unusable.

What survives is ONE usable report per failed institution in the 6-18 month
window. That is the real sample size, and it was not knowable without the
timestamps.

  DHFL                 FY2017-18   published 2018-07-19   10.5 months before
  Reliance Home Fin    FY2017-18   published 2018-09-20   11.3 months before
  SREI Infrastructure  FY2019-20   published 2020-08-28   13.2 months before
  Reliance Capital     FY2019-20   published 2020-06-01   17.9 months before

(Week one estimated SREI at "roughly 13 months". The exact figure is 13.2.)


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. IL&FS CANNOT BE TESTED AT ALL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
IL&FS Transportation Networks FY2016-17 (published 2017-09-04, 12.0 months
before the group default) contains NO maturity ladder. It discusses liquidity
risk in prose only.

The reason is structural, not a retrieval failure: ITNL is an infrastructure
developer, not a registered NBFC or HFC, so neither the RBI nor the NHB ALM
disclosure applies to it. The entity that actually defaulted on commercial
paper, IL&FS Financial Services, was never listed and files no annual report
to an exchange.

So of the three PRIMARY default cases, one is permanently outside the reach of
this data source. The usable PRIMARY sample is n=2.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. AUTOMATED EXTRACTION DOES NOT GENERALISE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The week-one parser read 2 of 5 pre-event filings. It reported "NO TABLE" for
three filings whose tables are plainly present on the page. Causes:

  DHFL              bucket headers capitalised as "Over 1 Year", defeating a
                    case-sensitive match
  Rel Home Finance  table transposed - buckets are ROWS, items are COLUMNS,
                    with prior-year figures interleaved in brackets
  Reliance Capital  two fiscal years interleaved as alternating lines within
                    each row, row labels wrapped across lines
  GIC Housing       non-standard font encoding; pdftotext returns mojibake
                    and all digits are lost

Across twelve filings that were read successfully there were at least five
distinct layouts and four different bucket schemas (RBI's 10-bucket NBFC
ladder; NHB's 10-bucket HFC ladder; Can Fin's 11-bucket variant; Bajaj's
8-bucket behavioural snapshot in the MD&A rather than the notes).

The figures in this analysis were therefore transcribed by hand from the
printed tables, with the source note and the units recorded per row in
alm_result.py. That is tractable for twelve filings. It is not tractable for
a hundred institutions over ten years without per-filer templates, and the
week-one claim that assembly was obstructed "not merely by tedium" is
confirmed in the stronger direction: it is obstructed by both.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. THE RESULT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Measure: cover = (inflows <= 1 year) / (outflows <= 1 year), from the filed
ladder. Lower is riskier. Compared only WITHIN cohort, because HFCs file on
the NHB schedule and other NBFCs on the RBI schedule.

COHORT A - housing finance companies, reports published mid-2018
  Repco Home Finance        0.222   -24.0%   survivor
  Can Fin Homes             0.326   -22.2%   survivor
  DHFL                      0.628    -8.3%   DEFAULTED 10.5 months later
  LIC Housing Finance       0.649    -8.0%   survivor
  PNB Housing Finance       0.797    -6.0%   survivor
  Reliance Home Finance     0.974    -0.5%   DEFAULTED 11.3 months later
  Indiabulls Housing        1.212    +4.4%   survivor (funding run 2019)

  AUC 0.400. Below chance. The two firms that defaulted rank 3rd and 6th of
  seven on riskiness. The two worst-looking firms in the cohort, Repco and
  Can Fin, are small conservative lenders that were never in difficulty.
  DHFL's ladder is indistinguishable from LIC Housing's: cover 0.628 against
  0.649, gap/assets -8.3% against -8.0%. LIC Housing is rated AAA today.

COHORT B - other NBFCs, reports published mid-2020
  SREI Infrastructure       0.143   -11.7%   board superseded 13.2 months later
  Cholamandalam             0.758    -7.1%   survivor
  M&M Financial             1.515   +13.6%   survivor
  Bajaj Finance             1.545   +16.3%   survivor
  Reliance Capital          2.614   +37.0%   board superseded 17.9 months later

  AUC 0.500. Exactly chance. The cohort's best-looking firm and its
  worst-looking firm are the two that failed.

Pre-registered baselines (alm_baseline.py), for completeness: the share of
borrowings maturing within one year (B4) scores AUC 0.000 in cohort A, and the
outflow/assets leverage proxy (B3) 0.100. Every measure, the ladder-based one
included, is at or below chance in this sample. Nothing here beats anything.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. WHY IT FAILS - THREE MECHANISMS, EVIDENCED FROM THE FILINGS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
These do not depend on the sample size. They are read directly off the pages.

A. THE GAP IS THE BUSINESS MODEL, NOT THE PATHOLOGY.
   All seven housing finance companies show a negative one-year gap. Borrowing
   short against twenty-year mortgages is what an HFC is. A measure on which
   every healthy firm in the industry also scores badly cannot separate
   within the industry. What killed DHFL was the loss of its ability to ROLL
   the gap after the IL&FS default froze the NBFC funding market - not the
   existence of the gap, which it had carried profitably for years and which
   LIC Housing carries today.
   Bajaj's own report notes that RBI permits a negative cumulative one-year
   mismatch of up to 15%. Four of the seven cohort-A firms exceed that, and
   none of those four failed.

B. THE DISCLOSURE IS DISCRETIONARY, AND BOTH FAILED FIRMS USED THE
   DISCRETION IN THE DIRECTION THAT FLATTERS THE GAP.
   Reliance Capital, FY2019-20, note (a): "All quoted investments have been
   included in 1 day to 30/31 days bucket considering its liquidity." Note
   (c): Rs 5,103 crore of debentures had ALREADY been recalled by the trustee
   and were "due and payable forthwith" at the balance-sheet date, with a
   further Rs 11,157 crore recalled shortly after - and "the management has
   presented the maturity pattern based on the original scheduled maturity
   payment dates."
   So the firm reported the BEST one-year cover in its cohort, 2.61, while
   already in default, by placing its holdings of subsidiaries in the
   one-month bucket and its accelerated debt at its original maturity.
   Reliance Home Finance, FY2017-18, note (b): asset timings rest on "best
   estimate of the Assets-Liability Committee / Management with regard to the
   timing of various cash flows and estimate of foreclosure of the loans
   expected in next one year" - pulling loan inflows forward. Its reported
   cover was 0.974, near-perfectly balanced, twelve months before default.
   This is the pre-registered falsification condition (2), confirmed with
   quotations rather than inferred.

C. THE FILING ENTITY IS OFTEN NOT THE ENTITY AT RISK.
   SREI Infrastructure Finance's standalone ladder reports ZERO advances and
   ZERO deposits: the lending book sat in SREI Equipment Finance. Its measured
   -11.7% describes a holding shell. Reliance Capital is a Core Investment
   Company - likewise a holding entity. Of the four testable failures, two are
   not the operating lender.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. A DESIGN FACT WORTH MORE THAN THE RESULT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
With 2 failures against 3 survivors, exhaustive enumeration of all 10 possible
label assignments gives a smallest attainable p-value of 0.100. Cohort B could
not have produced a conventionally significant result even with PERFECT
separation. Cohort A, at 2 against 5, bottoms out at 0.048 - significance
would have required the two defaulters to occupy exactly the two riskiest
ranks of seven.

This was knowable before any PDF was downloaded, from the event count alone.
Any future version of this study must either expand the event count
substantially (which section 2 shows is bounded by which entities file at all)
or abandon per-event significance testing and argue from mechanism instead.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7. WHAT THE WEEK-ONE DOCUMENT GOT WRONG
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- It reported SREI's gap as "-41,505" alongside Bajaj's "+21,128" as though
  both were crore. SREI's table is in Rs lakh; the gap is Rs 415 crore, not
  Rs 41,505 crore. The ratio to assets, -11.7%, was computed within the table
  and is unaffected, but the side-by-side comparison of absolute figures was
  wrong by a factor of 100.
- It presented SREI-versus-Bajaj as "pointing in the expected direction".
  With five more survivors in each cohort, the direction disappears.
- It treated the annual report as available at the fiscal year-end. Three of
  the reports it would have used were published after their events.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8. DECISION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STOP. The stop condition in OUTCOME_DEFINITION.txt section 6 required
recoverable pre-event observations for at least 3 PRIMARY cases on a common
basis. Only 2 PRIMARY cases are recoverable at all, they disagree with each
other, and the basis is not common - it is chosen by the filer, and the
failures chose the flattering one.

Spending the next six weeks extracting a hundred institutions would produce a
larger version of a measure that is at or below chance and whose inputs the
subject firms control. The ALM pivot is closed.

What this exercise is worth keeping for: it is a clean, pre-registered,
point-in-time-disciplined negative result with a mechanism explanation and
direct quotation from the filings. That is a more defensible contribution than
the positive result it was looking for, and it cost two days rather than two
months. The three mechanisms in section 5 - business-model confounding,
reporting discretion under stress, and entity-versus-group mismatch - are the
reusable finding, and the second of them is the same failure mode that
defeated the fundamental index on Yes Bank and the US model on Silicon Valley
Bank: accounts disclose what the filer is willing to admit.
