High-level workflow and low-level architecture

Version:  prototype, 2026-10-13
Code:     2,475 lines  (1,766 Python across 8 modules, 709 HTML/CSS/JS)
Corpus:   661 XBRL instances, 25,919 exchange announcements, 16 issuers
Cache:    47 MB on disk, 3.95 MB exported to the browser


================================================================================
PART ONE - HIGH LEVEL
================================================================================

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.1 WHAT THE PRODUCT IS FOR
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A credit analyst covering Indian non-bank lenders has to decide, repeatedly,
whether an issuer they hold or lend to is deteriorating. The evidence exists
and is public, but it is scattered across quarterly results, annual reports,
rating notices and ad-hoc exchange disclosures, and it arrives at
unpredictable times.

The product compresses the discovery and verification half of that job. It
does not attempt the judgement half.

    Stated so it can be falsified:
    "Reduce the time an analyst spends discovering, verifying and
     investigating emerging risk in Indian NBFCs."

    NOT: "predict defaults." That framing was tested and abandoned - see 1.5.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.2 THE WORKFLOW, END TO END
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

   [1] INGEST          Pull every public filing for each issuer from the
                       exchange, with the timestamp at which it was
                       disseminated. Cache to disk, never re-fetch.
                          |
   [2] PARSE           Turn tagged XBRL into figures keyed by their true
                       reporting period and reporting basis. Join annual
                       balance-sheet data and stamp it with the publication
                       date of the annual report that carried it.
                          |
   [3] DETECT          Run ten independent checks. Each emits a finding only
                       when a stated threshold is crossed, and each finding
                       carries its own counterexplanation.
                          |
   [4] ASSEMBLE        Sort by severity, collapse duplicates, separate
                       substantive findings from statements about the limits
                       of the evidence.
                          |
   [5] PRESENT         Issuer queue -> issuer file -> five views. Everything
                       filtered by one as-of date, which the analyst moves.
                          |
   [6] BRIEF           Emit what changed, why it may matter, what contradicts
                       it, and the questions a human must answer.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.3 WHAT THE ANALYST ACTUALLY DOES WITH IT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
TRIAGE.      The left rail ranks issuers by how many unexamined high-severity
             findings sit in their file. This is a reading queue, NOT a risk
             ranking. Bajaj Finance shows 2 findings over six years; Reliance
             Capital shows 37. But Repco Home Finance, which never got into
             difficulty, shows 14. Read the count as "how much is here",
             never as probability.

INVESTIGATE. Open an issuer and read the findings newest-first. Each states
             what changed, why it may matter, the innocent explanation, the
             source document, and what to check next.

VERIFY.      Every figure links to the XBRL instance or the PDF the exchange
             published. Nothing in the tool is the final authority; the
             linked document is.

REPLAY.      Move the as-of date to any point in the past. The entire file
             regenerates using only what had been published by then. This is
             how an analyst tests their own process - "what did our file on
             this issuer actually say on 31 May 2019?" - instead of reading
             today's file and concluding the failure was obvious.

HAND OVER.   The investigation brief is the artefact that leaves the tool and
             goes into a credit memo or a committee pack.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.4 THE FIVE VIEWS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Findings            Grouped by severity, newest first. The working surface.
Capital & leverage  Annual equity/assets, leverage, borrowings/assets, ROA,
                    gross NPA - each dated by when the annual report was
                    published, not by the fiscal year end.
Reported figures    Quarterly income, PBT, PAT, impairment exactly as filed,
                    with a filing-lag bar marked against the statutory limit.
                    Standalone and consolidated shown separately, never mixed.
Disclosure timeline Categorised announcement stream with links to source PDFs.
Investigation brief The prose summary, for pasting into a memo.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.5 THE DESIGN CONSTRAINTS, AND WHERE THEY CAME FROM
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Each of these is a response to a specific negative result from the research
phase that preceded the product. They are constraints, not preferences.

NO COMPOSITE SCORE.
    A nine-component weighted distress index did not beat plain equity/assets
    on held-out US bank data. Where a simple ratio beats a composite, show the
    simple ratio. The tool ranks evidence and leaves weighting to the analyst.

PUBLICATION DATE, NEVER PERIOD END.
    Checking dissemination timestamps deleted three of five cases from the
    earlier study. DHFL's FY2019 annual report was published 5 September 2019,
    three months AFTER its 4 June default. Any screen joining on fiscal year
    would "predict" the default from a document published after it.

NEVER MIX REPORTING BASIS.
    SREI Infrastructure's standalone accounts showed zero advances because the
    loan book sat in a subsidiary. The listed entity is frequently not the
    entity at risk.

NEVER IMPUTE.
    A missing figure produces a coverage finding, not a filled value. A
    mis-scaled filing is excluded and reported, not rescaled by a guessed
    multiplier.

ALWAYS STATE THE INNOCENT EXPLANATION.
    Shriram Finance's year-to-date income genuinely does not reconcile with
    its quarters - because a merger restated the comparatives. A detector that
    flags without explaining trains the user to ignore it.

ASSUME THE FIGURES MAY BE FALSE.
    Reliance Capital reported the best one-year liquidity cover in its peer
    group while Rs 5,103 crore of its debentures had already been recalled by
    the trustee. The tool therefore leans on signals that do not depend on the
    figures being true - above all, whether the issuer filed on time.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.2 DATA SOURCES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
All three NSE endpoints are unauthenticated but require a browser User-Agent
and a Referer header. urllib hangs on them; curl via subprocess does not, so
every fetch shells out.

  (a) /api/corporate-announcements?index=equities&symbol=<SYM>
      Categorised disclosure stream, 2004 onwards.
      Fields used: an_dt (announcement timestamp), desc (category),
      attchmntText (summary), attchmntFile (source PDF), exchdisstime.
      Volume: 601 (RHFL) to 3,934 (Shriram) per issuer; 25,919 total.
      AVAILABLE FOR DELISTED ISSUERS - DHFL returns 1,276 items.

  (b) /api/corporates-financial-results?index=equities&symbol=<SYM>&period=Quarterly
      Index of every result filing back to ~2007.
      Fields used: broadCastDate, filingDate, consolidated (Standalone /
      Consolidated), audited, relatingTo, xbrl (URL of the instance).

  (c) The XBRL instance named in (b).
      Tagged, machine-readable. No PDF parsing anywhere in the pipeline.
      Only available from ~2019, when it became mandatory - see 2.9.

  (d) /api/annual-reports?index=equities&symbol=<SYM>
      Used by annual.py only, for publication timestamps.

  (e) ../risk_engine/panel.csv  (local archive, 6,224 institution-years)
      Annual balance sheets 2012-2023. Joined on the `nse` column.
      ALIAS = {"SAMMAANCAP": "IBULHSGFIN"} handles one rename.

2.3 CACHE LAYOUT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  cache/
    <SYMBOL>/
      announcements.json      raw response (a)
      results.json            raw response (b)

rewrites it, and every figure the UI shows names the file it came from.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.4 XBRL PARSING  (facts.py)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Regex extraction, not an XML tree - the instances are flat and the tree adds
nothing.

    FACT = <in-bse-fin:(\w+) contextRef="(One|Two|...|Six)([A-Za-z0-9]*)" ...>

One instance carries several COLUMNS of the published results table,
distinguished by context prefix: One = the quarter, Four = the year to date,
and so on.

THE CRITICAL DETAIL: the xbrli:context period dates are unreliable. In a Bajaj
Q3 filing every context is stamped 2024-10-01 to 2024-12-31, including the
nine-month column. The true period comes instead from the tagged facts
DateOfStartOfReportingPeriod / DateOfEndOfReportingPeriod belonging to each
prefix, which are correct. Using the context dates would silently merge
quarterly and cumulative figures.

Span classification: <=100 days = Q, <=190 = H, <=285 = 9M, else FY.

Captured: 23 metrics (interest income through capital adequacy) and 9 metadata
fields, of which three matter most -
    NatureOfReportStandaloneConsolidated
    WhetherResultsAreAuditedOrUnaudited
    DeclarationOfUnmodifiedOpinionOrStatementOfImpactOfAuditQualification

Values are absolute rupees, NOT the scale named in
LevelOfRoundingUsedInFinancialStatements - see 2.6 (c).

load_symbol() joins each instance to its index row so every column knows its
dissemination timestamp.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.5 THE FINDING RECORD
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Every detector emits the same shape, via F():

    symbol            issuer
    kind              detector that produced it
    severity          high | medium | low
    knowable_on       ISO timestamp - when a diligent analyst could first
                      have seen it. For a missed deadline this is the day the
                      DEADLINE PASSED, not the day the issuer finally filed.
    what_changed      one sentence with the figures in it
    why_it_matters    the mechanism, stated as hypothesis
    evidence          dict: source, file, url, timestamp, basis, intermediate
                      values used in the test
    investigate       what the human does next
    counterevidence   the innocent explanation, or null
    metric, value     machine-readable payload

`knowable_on` is the axis the entire product turns on. Every filter, the
replay control, and the brief all key off it.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.6 THE TEN DETECTORS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

(a) check_filing_timeliness          -> filing_overdue
    SEBI LODR Reg 33: 45 days after a quarter, 60 after year end.
    RELAXED = {"2020-03-31": 122, "2020-06-30": 77} encodes SEBI's actual
    COVID-19 circulars rather than apologising for them afterwards. Omitting
    this put four spurious findings on LIC Housing.
    Severity by days overdue: >=30 high, >=10 medium, else low.
    THE STRONGEST SIGNAL IN THE CORPUS. DHFL filed its audited FY2019 results
    114 days after period end; the default fell inside that gap. RHFL filed
    136 days late against its own ~50-day norm, 18 days before defaulting.

(b) check_additivity                 -> additivity_break
    YTD(n) must equal the sum of the quarters inside it. Pure arithmetic on
    filed figures.
    Fires when |diff| > max(1% of YTD, Rs 5 crore) and |YTD| >= Rs 50 crore.
    Both floors are needed: the first build, with a relative test only,
    reported a Rs 0.3 crore "restatement" three times.

(c) check_scale / scale_anomalies    -> unit_anomaly
    A minority of instances are pre-scaled and the tagged rounding field does
    not predict which - Shriram filed absolute figures under "Lakhs" while LIC
    Housing filed scaled figures under the same label.
    Two refinements were required:
      - compare against a LOCAL window of +/-4 neighbouring filings, because a
        global median called 17 Reliance Capital filings mis-tagged when the
        company had genuinely collapsed to shell scale;
      - compare QUARTER columns only, because pooling year-to-date columns
        inflated the median and let a LIC Housing filing a hundred times too
        small pass;
      - require the ratio to sit within 45% of a clean factor
        (1e-7, 1e-5, 1e-2, 1e2, 1e5, 1e7), because a tagging error produces an
        exact factor and a business collapse does not.
    Suspect columns are EXCLUDED from every numeric detector and reported.

(d) check_basis_availability         -> basis_withdrawn
    Consolidated reporting present one year and absent the next.

(e) check_audit_flags                -> audit_opinion
    The tagged audit-qualification field, where it is not "unmodified" or
    "not applicable".

(f) check_earnings, profit branch    -> deterioration
    Fires ONLY on an actual reversal into loss against a trailing
    four-quarter median profit. A decline in profit is not reported: the
    first build flagged 13 "deteriorations" at Bajaj Finance, which grew
    throughout.

(g) check_earnings, credit-cost branch -> deterioration
    Impairment compared with THE SAME QUARTER OF THE PREVIOUS YEAR, because
    NBFC provisioning is strongly seasonal and a trailing-median comparison
    mostly detects Q4. Requires >=50% increase.

    Materiality floor for (f) and (g) scales with the issuer:
        max(Rs 5 crore, 2% of trailing four-quarter total income)
    A Rs 5 crore move matters for Repco and is noise for Bajaj.

(h) check_capital                    -> capital_erosion
    Annual equity/assets falling >=25% relative, or turning negative.
    Featured on its own because equity/assets out-performed the nine-component
    index on held-out data in the research phase.

(i) check_announcements              -> rating_action, exchange_clarification,
                                        price_flag, disclosure_burst
    Three NSE categories are watched. An announcement becomes a FINDING only
    if its text matches DISTRESS_RE (default, downgrade, rating watch,
    moratorium, NCLT, forensic, going concern, auditor resignation, ...);
    otherwise it stays on the timeline only.
    Without this gate the first build emitted 133 findings for DHFL, one per
    routine rating announcement. The matched phrase is recorded in the
    evidence as `promoted_because_text_matched`, so the promotion is auditable.
    disclosure_burst fires on >=4 matched items within 30 days.

(j) check_coverage                   -> coverage_gap, stale_evidence
    Missing quarters, and days since the last parseable filing. These are
    statements about the EVIDENCE, not the issuer, and the UI lists them
    under their own heading.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.7 DEDUPLICATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Two distinct duplications, both of which inflated the first build:

  (a) The exchange files one event twice - its own query and the issuer's
      reply arrive as separate announcements. Collapsed on
      (kind, day, first 90 chars of what_changed).

  (b) An issuer whose consolidated figures barely differ from standalone
      generates the identical finding under both bases. LIC Housing produced
      18 findings where 9 were warranted. These are MERGED, and the surviving
      finding appends "The same figure was filed on both the standalone and
      consolidated basis" - because agreement across bases is information,
      whereas two identical cards are not.

_strip_basis() removes the basis marker before computing the signature.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.8 POINT-IN-TIME REPLAY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The whole corpus is exported with every finding's knowable_on, and the filter
is a string comparison in the browser:

    visible(issuer) = findings where knowable_on[:10] <= as_of

Three consequences, all deliberate:

  - Instant. No re-query, no server.
  - The outcome banner is WITHHELD while as_of precedes the outcome date, so
    the replay cannot be read with hindsight.
  - Nothing in detect.py reads universe.py's outcome field, so the findings
    themselves are blind to who failed.

Demonstration: set as-of to 2019-05-31 and the rail ranks Reliance Home
Finance, Reliance Capital and DHFL first, second and third. All three failed
within thirty months. This is a demonstration, not evidence - three cases
chosen with hindsight in a universe of sixteen.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.9 ENFORCING POINT-IN-TIME INTEGRITY  (check_pit.py)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The central claim - a finding dated D rests only on evidence published on or
before D - is worthless if merely asserted. It breaks by accident, and it did.
Two leaks were found in this codebase by writing the test rather than by
reading the code:

  (a) disclosure_burst scanned 30 days FORWARD but stamped the finding with
      the first item in the window. At as-of = window start the finding was
      visible although the announcements establishing it had not yet
      happened. Now dated at the LAST item - when the cluster completed.

  (b) scale_anomalies compared each filing against +/-4 neighbours, so
      whether a filing was usable depended on filings published later, and
      that decision propagated into every numeric detector. Now a TRAILING
      window only. The cost is that an issuer's first few filings cannot be
      scale-checked; that is the correct trade.

A third case was subtler and is a design point rather than a bug.
filing_overdue is dated at the DEADLINE, which is right: on that day an
analyst can observe that nothing has been filed. But the finding's text said
"filed 114 days after period end", and the eventual lag is not knowable on
the deadline. The resolution:

    knowable at the deadline   ->  what_changed, and evidence keys
                                   period_end, statutory_limit_days,
                                   deadline, trailing_median_lag_days
    knowable only later        ->  evidence keys prefixed retrospective_
                                   (retrospective_filed_on, _lag_days,
                                    _days_past_limit, _file, _url)

The UI renders the retrospective half only once the as-of date has passed it,
so the same finding reads "as at 2019-05-31, 1 further day has passed with
nothing filed" before the filing arrives and "it was eventually filed on
2019-07-23, 114 days after period end" after.

check_pit.py walks every finding of every issuer, extracts every ISO date in
its evidence payload, and fails if any postdates the finding's own
knowable_on. Keys prefixed retrospective_ are exempt by convention, as are
the two kinds that describe the evidence rather than the issuer
(coverage_gap, stale_evidence) and are stamped "today" by construction.

    $ python3 check_pit.py
    findings scanned        : 289
    evidence dates checked  : 610
    violations              : 0

Run it after any change to detect.py. Exit status 1 on any violation.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.10 KNOWN LIMITS, IN THE CODE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- XBRL exists only from ~2019. DHFL has 14 readable filings against 73 result
  filings listed. Earlier periods would need the results PDF.
- No rating level or history. NSE's /api/corporate-credit-rating returns an
  empty array for every issuer tested. Rating ACTIONS appear only as free text
  in the announcement stream. This is the largest functional gap.
- /api/corporates-financial-results-data returns "no data found" for
  pre-XBRL filings, so there is no structured fallback.
- DHFL is absent from panel.csv entirely, so it has no Capital & leverage
  view. It was delisted before the archive snapshot.
- Asset quality is thin. gross NPA is present for only 6 of the 15 issuers
  with annual data (Bajaj, PNB Housing, IIFL, Muthoot, Can Fin, Repco) and
  absent for the other nine, including every failed issuer. Two reasons: a
  core investment company such as Reliance Capital books no loans of its own,
  and the archive simply does not carry the field for every filer - across the
  whole panel only 629 of 6,223 institution-years have it. The Capital &
  leverage view OMITS a column no row populates and states the omission,
  rather than printing a column of placeholders, which reads as a broken
  table. Treat the gap as unknown, never as zero.
- 15 of 16 issuers have annual data; 19 of 176 annual observations carry an
  ESTIMATED publication date (year end + 6 months) because no annual report
  could be matched. These are flagged "est" in the UI and in the evidence.
- NSE's own feed contains U+FFFD replacement characters where apostrophes
  were lost upstream. clean() renders a run between two word characters as an
  apostrophe and drops the rest. Presentation only; cached bytes are never
  altered.
- No unlisted entity is covered. IL&FS Financial Services, which defaulted on
  the commercial paper, was never listed and files nothing.
- 16 issuers. India has over 9,000 registered NBFCs.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.11 FRONT END
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
One HTML file, 687 lines, no framework and no build step. Loads
workbench_data.js, which assigns window.WB.

  State:   { sym, asof, tab }  - three fields, re-rendered by one draw().
  Render:  innerHTML from template strings; all interpolated text passes
           through esc().
  Theme:   CSS custom properties on :root, redefined under
           prefers-color-scheme and again under [data-theme] so an explicit
           toggle wins in both directions. Choice persisted in localStorage
           inside try/catch.
  Charts:  Hand-drawn with divs, no chart library. Two bespoke marks -
             - a filing-lag bar with a tick at the statutory limit and the
               over-limit portion in the high-severity colour;
             - a diverging equity/assets bar with a zero rule.
  Tables:  wrapped in overflow-x:auto; tabular-nums throughout.

Export is 3.95 MB. Deployed as a two-file artifact (page + data).


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.12 RUNNING IT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
    python3 ingest.py              # all issuers, resumable, ~25 min cold
    python3 ingest.py BAJFINANCE   # one issuer
    python3 facts.py               # parse check
    python3 annual.py              # annual layer -> annual.json
    python3 detect.py              # finding counts per issuer
    python3 check_pit.py           # point-in-time integrity test (must pass)
    python3 brief.py DHFL 2019-05-31   # text brief at a past date
    python3 build.py               # -> workbench_data.json

    python3 -m http.server 8777 --directory credit_workbench
    # then http://localhost:8777/workbench.html
    # file:// will NOT work - the data file is loaded as a sibling script

To add an issuer: append to ISSUERS in universe.py, run ingest.py <SYM>,
then build.py. Nothing else is hard-coded per issuer except the RELATED
pairs and the panel.csv ALIAS.
