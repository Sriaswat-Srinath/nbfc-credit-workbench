NBFC CREDIT WORKBENCH - WHAT IS BUILT, WHAT IS NOT, AND HOW TO TEST IT
================================================================================
Date: 2026-10-13
Status: Working prototype over 16 issuers. Not validated with users.

Run it:   python3 -m http.server 8777 --directory credit_workbench
          then open http://localhost:8777/workbench.html
Rebuild:  python3 ingest.py && python3 build.py


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. THE PROPOSITION, STATED SO IT CAN BE FALSIFIED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
"Reduce the time a credit analyst spends discovering, verifying and
investigating emerging risk in Indian NBFCs."

Not "predict defaults". The research that preceded this tool established why
that framing was abandoned:
  - A nine-component weighted distress index did NOT beat plain equity/assets
    on held-out US bank data.
  - A US model caught 86% of bank failures by count and 4% by assets; it
    missed Silicon Valley Bank entirely, whose last call report was healthy on
    every measure.
  - An ALM maturity-gap screen scored AUC 0.400 and 0.500 against matched
    Indian cohorts - at or below chance - and the firm that reported the
    HEALTHIEST one-year liquidity position in its peer group was already in
    default on recalled debentures.

So this tool has no composite score, by design. It ranks evidence and leaves
the judgement to a person.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. THE TWO THINGS THAT ARE ACTUALLY DIFFERENTIATED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Everything else here is table stakes that Informist, The Company Check and
ICRA Analytics already sell. These two are not, and both came out of the
research rather than out of a feature list.

(A) PUBLICATION-DATE DISCIPLINE, AND A REPLAY BUILT ON IT
    Every figure and every finding is stamped with the moment it reached the
    exchange, never the fiscal period it describes. The as-of control then
    regenerates the entire file as it read on any past date.

    This is not a nicety. Checking those timestamps deleted three of five
    cases from the earlier study: DHFL's FY2019 annual report was published
    5 September 2019, three months AFTER its 4 June default. A conventional
    screen that joins on fiscal year would have "predicted" the default using
    a document published after it.

    The commercial use is process testing: an analyst can ask "what did our
    file on this issuer actually say on 31 May 2019?" instead of reading
    today's file and concluding the failure was obvious.

(B) CONTRADICTION DETECTION BY ARITHMETIC ON FILED DATA
    Year-to-date figures must equal the sum of their quarters. Where they do
    not, a quarter was restated after it was first published. The tool does
    that arithmetic across filings and shows both sides with their filing
    dates.

    Worked example in the data: Shriram Finance's nine-month total income for
    FY2023 was filed as Rs 22,542 crore while the three quarters inside it, as
    originally filed, sum to Rs 18,310 crore. The tool flags it AND names the
    likely innocent cause - the Shriram City Union merger restating
    comparatives. That is the intended behaviour: find the contradiction,
    offer the counterexplanation, send a human to check.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. THE FIVE FUNCTIONS, HONESTLY GRADED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. ISSUER PROFILE                                   PARTIAL
   Built: quarterly P&L history, annual balance sheet, related listed
   entities, lender type, group.
   Missing: rating history. NSE's corporate-credit-rating endpoint returns
   empty for every issuer tested. Rating ACTIONS appear in the announcement
   stream as free text, but there is no structured rating level or history.
   This is the largest gap and it matters, because the honest commercial
   question is whether this beats reading the rating agency's commentary.

2. DISCLOSURE TIMELINE                              BUILT
   2004 onwards, categorised by NSE, with dissemination timestamps and links
   to the original PDF. 1,276 announcements for DHFL alone - and it is fully
   available for delisted issuers, which was not obvious before testing.

3. EVIDENCE-LINKED FINANCIAL ANALYSIS               BUILT
   Quarterly: total income, PBT, PAT, impairment, from tagged XBRL - no PDF
   parsing. Annual: equity/assets, leverage, borrowings/assets, ROA, gross
   NPA. Every figure shows its source file, its basis, and its timestamp.
   Standalone and consolidated are never mixed.

4. CHANGE AND INCONSISTENCY DETECTION               BUILT - the strongest part
   Nine detectors: missed filing deadline, year-to-date reconciliation break,
   inconsistent reporting scale, consolidated reporting withdrawn, non-clean
   audit opinion, capital erosion, swing into loss, year-on-year credit-cost
   jump, and promoted announcements (rating actions and exchange
   clarification requests matching distress language).
   Each distinguishes genuine contradiction from legitimate accounting
   difference by carrying an explicit counterexplanation.

5. ANALYST INVESTIGATION BRIEF                      BUILT
   What changed, why it may matter, the evidence, the counterevidence, and
   the questions a human has to answer. It does not conclude.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. THE SIGNAL THAT SURPRISED ME, AND IS WORTH LEADING THE DEMO WITH
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The strongest discriminator in the data is not a ratio. It is whether the
issuer filed on time.

  DHFL   audited FY2019 results due 30 May 2019, filed 23 July 2019.
         114 days after period end. The first default fell inside that gap,
         on 4 June.
  RHFL   audited FY2019 results filed 136 days after period end against the
         issuer's own trailing norm of about 50 days, and 18 days before its
         1 September 2019 default.

A missed deadline is one of the few signals that does not depend on the
reported figures being true, which is exactly the weakness that defeated
every model in the research phase.

Set the as-of control to 31 May 2019 and the rail ranks Reliance Home
Finance, Reliance Capital and DHFL first, second and third. All three failed
within thirty months. Nothing in the detection logic reads the outcome
labels, and the outcome banner is withheld until the as-of date passes the
event.

This is a demonstration, not evidence. Three cases, chosen with hindsight,
in a universe of sixteen.

5. ALERT RATES - THE NUMBER TO JUDGE THE TOOL BY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Substantive findings over the full coverage period, today's date:

  Reliance Capital      37     SREI Infrastructure   30
  Reliance Home Fin     25     Edelweiss             19
  DHFL                  17     Repco Home Finance    14
  Sammaan (Indiabulls)  12     LIC Housing           10
  IIFL Finance          11     Muthoot               12
  PNB Housing           10     Manappuram             7
  Shriram Finance       10     Can Fin Homes          5
  Cholamandalam          5     Bajaj Finance          2

Bajaj Finance over six years produces two findings. Reliance Capital produces
thirty-seven. The separation is real but it is NOT a prediction: Repco Home
Finance, a conservative lender that never got into trouble, sits at fourteen.
Read the count as "how much is here to look at", never as risk.

Getting here took three rounds of tightening. The first build emitted 133
findings for DHFL, including one per routine rating announcement, and three
false restatements caused by rounding noise on figures under Rs 1 crore. The
thresholds that fixed it are visible constants at the top of detect.py, not
hidden in a model, so an analyst can disagree with them.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. KNOWN LIMITS - SAY THESE BEFORE A PROSPECT FINDS THEM
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Structured quarterly results exist only from about 2019, when XBRL became
  mandatory. DHFL has 14 readable filings against 73 result filings listed.
  Earlier periods need the PDF.
- No rating data, as above.
- No unlisted entity. IL&FS Financial Services, which actually defaulted on
  the commercial paper, was never listed and files nothing. The research
  phase also found IL&FS Transportation files no maturity ladder at all
  because it is not a registered NBFC. The riskiest entity in a group is
  often the one that discloses least.
- Some NSE XBRL instances are mis-scaled, and the tagged rounding field does
  not predict which. The tool detects and EXCLUDES them rather than guessing
  a multiplier, and reports the exclusion.
- Everything here derives from what the issuer chose to disclose. Reliance
  Capital reported the best one-year liquidity cover in its peer group while
  Rs 5,103 crore of its debentures had already been recalled.
- Sixteen issuers. India has over 9,000 registered NBFCs.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7. WHAT TO ASK FIVE ANALYSTS - AND WHAT A REAL SIGNAL LOOKS LIKE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Open with their last investigation, not with the demo:

  1. Walk me through the last issuer you investigated because something
     looked wrong. What made you look?
  2. Which sources did you open, in what order, and how long did it take?
  3. What was hardest to reconcile?
  4. Was there anything you found out later than you would have liked?
  5. What do you use now, and what does your firm pay for it?

Then show the tool and ask:
  6. Does a missed filing deadline already reach you? How?
  7. If a year-to-date figure did not reconcile with the quarters, would you
     want to know, or is that noise?
  8. Would you trust a brief you did not assemble yourself? What would have
     to be true for you to?

A real signal is NOT enthusiasm. It is an analyst who gives you a live
non-confidential workflow, uses the prototype on an issuer you did not
choose, and comes back unprompted. Record whether they can name a budget and
who signs.

Measure: research time with and without; whether every material claim could
be verified against source; findings that mattered versus findings ignored;
how often it failed for want of data; repeat use; willingness to pay.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8. MY HONEST READ ON THE COMMERCIAL QUESTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The timeline and the issuer profile are commodity - three vendors already
sell them, better resourced. The defensible part is narrow: point-in-time
reconstruction and arithmetic contradiction detection, both of which exist
because the research phase was forced to confront what publication lag and
reporting discretion do to a model.

That narrowness is the risk and the opportunity. It is probably not a
standalone product. It is plausibly a feature someone who already sells
monitoring would want, or a tool for model validation and process review
inside a lender or a regulator, where "what was knowable on this date" is the
actual question being asked.

Find that out from the five interviews before building anything further. Do
not expand the universe past sixteen issuers until someone has used it twice.
