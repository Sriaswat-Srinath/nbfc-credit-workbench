   2. The design assumed annual reports are available at fiscal year-end.

Date:    2026-10-12
Purpose: Go / no-go on whether an ALM maturity-mismatch screen for Indian NBFCs
         can be built from public data. NOT a model, NOT a result.

VERDICT: AMBER-GREEN. Proceed to a small panel, but three of the four
         assumptions behind the original pitch were wrong or unproven.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. WHAT WAS CLAIMED, AND WHAT THE TEST FOUND
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

CLAIM 1  "There is no Texas Ratio for ALM mismatch."
STATUS   WRONG. Withdrawn before testing. RBI's NBFC ALM framework specifies
         maturity ladders and cumulative gap measurement; ICRA's published
         rating methodology explicitly uses near-term cumulative mismatches.
         The opportunity is a public, longitudinal, cross-institutional
         implementation of a measure that already exists privately - a much
         narrower claim that must be demonstrated, not assumed.

CLAIM 2  "The data is disclosed but unused."
STATUS   PARTLY TRUE, and harder than implied. The table exists. Assembling it
         into a comparable panel is obstructed by schema and basis differences
         (see section 3), not merely by tedium.

CLAIM 3  "Nobody has assembled it, which is the moat."
STATUS   UNPROVEN. Not tested here, and not testable from the data. Tedium is
         not a moat if a rating agency already does it internally.

CLAIM 4  "Failures are frequent enough to validate."
STATUS   UNPROVEN and conflates event types. Rating downgrades, CP market
         exit, missed payments and resolution are different outcomes and must
         be labelled separately rather than pooled into one distress flag.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. DATA FEASIBILITY - THE THREE QUESTIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Q1. Can annual reports be located, with a verifiable publication date?
    YES.
    NSE publishes a per-symbol annual-report index:
        https://www.nseindia.com/api/annual-reports?index=equities&symbol=<SYM>
    Returns up to 17 reports per company, back beyond 2018, each carrying
    `broadcast_dttm` - the actual dissemination timestamp.
      Bajaj Finance FY2019 report  broadcast 29-JUN-2019 19:13:52
      Bajaj Finance FY2020 report  broadcast 29-JUN-2020 21:14:28
    This is the point-in-time anchor the design requires. Screener.in also
    links annual reports but keeps only recent years for active companies,
    so it cannot supply a matched-period control group. Use NSE.

    Gotcha: some NSE "PDF" URLs are ZIP archives with a .pdf extension and
    must be unzipped before parsing. Silent failure otherwise - pdftotext
    returns zero characters.

Q2. Does the RBI maturity table appear, in parseable form?
    YES, for the cases tested.
      SREI Infrastructure FY2020   10 buckets, contractual, 6 rows
      Bajaj Finance      FY2019     8 buckets, behavioural, 6 rows
      Reliance Capital   FY2018     partial (2 rows parsed)
      Reliance Home Fin  FY2021     partial (2 rows parsed)
    Rows follow the RBI schedule: deposits, advances, investments, borrowings,
    foreign-currency assets and liabilities.

Q3. Is the format consistent enough for a panel?
    NO, not without normalisation. See section 3.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. THE OBSTACLES, NAMED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A. BUCKET SCHEMAS DIFFER
   SREI uses the 10-bucket RBI ladder (1-7d, 8-14d, 15-30d, ...).
   Bajaj uses 8 buckets, starting "Up to 1 month".
   Reconcilable - Bajaj's first bucket is the sum of SREI's first three - but
   every filer must be mapped onto a common ladder before comparison.

B. BASIS DIFFERS, AND THIS IS NOT COSMETIC
   Bajaj's table is footnoted "Amounts disclosed as per the behavouralised
   pattern." SREI's is contractual. RBI permits both. A behavioural pattern
   assumes deposits roll and loans prepay at historical rates; a contractual
   one does not. The two answer different questions and a cross-sectional
   comparison that ignores the distinction is invalid.
   -> The basis footnote must be captured as a field, and comparisons made
      within basis, or the behavioural ones adjusted.

C. STANDALONE VERSUS CONSOLIDATED
   SREI Infrastructure's FY2020 standalone table shows ZERO advances: the
   lending book had been transferred to SREI Equipment Finance. The listed
   entity's own table therefore understates group risk badly.
   -> Record which basis was found. Prefer consolidated. Where the operating
      company is a subsidiary, the parent's table is not a substitute.

D. PARTIAL PARSES
   Reliance Capital and Reliance Home Finance yielded only 2 of 6 rows. Cause
   not yet diagnosed - likely table layout or line wrapping in those filings.
   Any panel build needs a per-extraction completeness flag, and rows that
   fail should be left missing rather than inferred.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. THE ONE SIGNAL OBSERVATION, WITH ITS CAVEATS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Cumulative gap to one year, as filed:

  institution                       inflow<=1y   outflow<=1y        gap   gap/assets
  Bajaj Finance FY2019 (survivor)       52,762        31,634    +21,128      +19.6%
  SREI Infra FY2020 (failed 10/2021)     6,921        48,426    -41,505      -11.7%

SREI: 100% of borrowings fell due within one year against 2% of investments,
disclosed in a document broadcast 13.2 months before the RBI superseded its
board (exact publication date 2020-08-28, verified in week two).

  NOTE: the two rows above are NOT in the same units. SREI's table is in Rs
  lakh and Bajaj's in Rs crore. Corrected, SREI's gap is Rs 415 crore. With
  five more survivors added to each cohort in week two, the apparent direction
  disappears entirely: cohort AUC 0.400 and 0.500.

THIS IS NOT EVIDENCE. It is two institutions, in different fiscal years, on
different bucket schemas, on different bases (behavioural vs contractual), one
of which is a near-empty standalone shell. It establishes that the measure
computes and points in the expected direction. Nothing more.

The retrospective-explanation trap is live here: knowing SREI failed, a large
mismatch is easy to find. The question that matters is whether the mismatch
separates future failures from ordinary variation ACROSS a population, before
the event, at an alert rate someone can work through.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. WHAT WOULD HAVE TO HAPPEN NEXT, IN ORDER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

STEP 1  Define the target before extracting anything further.
        Primary outcome: publicly documented debt-payment default within 12
        months. Secondary, labelled separately: rating downgrade or watch,
        refinancing difficulty, regulatory intervention, resolution.

STEP 2  Build the extraction with provenance per observation:
        fiscal period | broadcast_dttm | source URL | page | basis
        (contractual/behavioural) | standalone/consolidated | bucket schema |
        completeness flag. An observation without a broadcast timestamp
        earlier than the event cannot support any claim of advance warning.

STEP 3  Normalise bucket schemas onto a common ladder; compare within basis.

STEP 4  Baselines, pre-registered, before looking at results:
        existing FDI | equity/assets | leverage | short-term borrowings share |
        a naive maturity gap | rating level where available.
        The test is INCREMENTAL information, not association with distress.

STEP 5  Evaluate operationally: recall at a fixed alert rate, false alerts per
        institution-year, median warning time, performance in market-wide
        stress, and a genuinely later holdout. Report intervals; n will be
        small.

STEP 6  Do not tune on SREI, DHFL and IL&FS and then present those same three
        as validation. If they shape the metric, the holdout must be other
        institutions or later events.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. KNOWN REMAINING UNKNOWNS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- DHFL and IL&FS were not retrieved in this test. RESOLVED in week two:
  DHFL's reports are served by NSE's annual-report API despite delisting, and
  its FY2017-18 ladder was recovered and tested. IL&FS is NOT resolvable -
  IL&FS Transportation files no maturity ladder because it is not a registered
  NBFC or HFC, and IL&FS Financial Services, the entity that defaulted, was
  never listed.
- Whether disclosed maturity buckets were accurate for firms heading into
  distress is unknown and material. The same "accounts only show what they
  admit" problem that defeated the index on Yes Bank applies here, and a
  firm under funding stress has an incentive to present the behavioural view.
- Whether any of this beats simply reading the rating agency's liquidity
  commentary is untested, and is the real commercial question.
