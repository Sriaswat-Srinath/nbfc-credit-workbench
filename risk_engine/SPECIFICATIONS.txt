================================================================================
BANK & NBFC DEFAULT RISK INTELLIGENCE SYSTEM
High-Level and Low-Level Specifications
================================================================================
Version:  2026-10-12
Scope:    Two parallel systems - Indian banks/NBFCs (primary) and US banks
          (external validation and native rebuild)
Status:   Research prototype. Not validated for consequential decisions.


================================================================================
PART A - HIGH-LEVEL SPECIFICATION
================================================================================

A1. PURPOSE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Estimate, from public data only, which lending institutions are financially
fragile, and whether that fragility is worsening - early enough and selectively
enough to be worth someone's attention.

The system produces a RANKED WATCHLIST, not a probability of default. An
institution scoring 88 is worse than 88% of comparable institutions that period.
It is not 88% likely to fail.


A2. WHAT IT DOES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Ingests public financial statements and market prices.
2. Computes six to nine risk ratios per institution per period.
3. Converts each ratio to a percentile against comparable institutions.
4. Combines them into a 0-100 distress index.
5. Raises an alert on a high level OR a sharp move against the institution's
   own history.
6. For listed institutions, independently estimates a Merton distance-to-default
   from equity prices.
7. Surfaces the disagreement between the accounting and market readings.
8. Backtests everything against dated, public distress events.


A3. WHAT IT DOES NOT DO
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- It does not produce calibrated default probabilities.
- It does not see interest-rate or duration risk (see D3 - this caused total
  failure on the 2023 US bank failures).
- It does not see group or related-party exposure, the mechanism behind IL&FS
  and Reliance Capital.
- It does not model sovereign or parent support, so state-backed institutions
  score as though unsupported.
- It contains no machine learning. Weights are set by economic reasoning.
- It cannot see what the filings do not report (Yes Bank's unrecognised loans).

A5. THE TWO ANALYSES, AND WHY BOTH
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  ANALYSIS 1 - Fundamental Distress Index
    Reads   : published annual accounts
    Covers  : all 563 Indian institutions
    Fails   : when the accounts conceal the problem

  ANALYSIS 2 - Merton structural credit risk
    Reads   : daily equity prices and debt
    Covers  : 153 listed institutions only
    Fails   : when there is no liquid market, or when prices embed state support

The failure mode of each is the strength of the other. Yes Bank is the worked
case: its accounts scored 69 while the market priced a one-in-three chance of
default four months before the moratorium.


A6. VALIDATION SUMMARY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  INDIA     18 dated events (13 RBI PCA placements, 5 credit events)
            17/18 alerted before the event; 14/18 within 24 months
            95% CI on recall 52-94%
            7/7 held-out events caught by thresholds derived without them
            Permutation p = 0.0003 (held-out only)

  US        563 failures; 529 development (2008-12), 63 held-out (2013-2023)
            54/63 caught by count (86%), median lead 15.8 months
            Watchlist 2.8% of banks, precision 24.8x base rate
            Permutation p < 0.001
            BY ASSETS: 4%. See D3.

PART B - LOW-LEVEL SPECIFICATION: INDIAN SYSTEM
================================================================================

B1. DATA INGESTION  (build_panel.py)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Input   : <archive>/<Company Name>/{Yearly_Balance_Sheet, Yearly_Profit_Loss,
          Yearly_Cash_flow, Ratios, <Name>_Basic_Info}.csv
Filter  : Basic_Info.Sector in {Banks, Finance}  -> 39 + 517 = 556 institutions
          plus 7 recovered -> 563
Headers : accepts "YYYY-MM-01" and "Mon YYYY"; retains March columns only
          (the Sep-2023 interim column is a partial period and is dropped)
Schema  : two P&L layouts are unified -
            revenue   <- "Revenue"          or "Sales"
            op_profit <- "Financing Profit" or "Operating Profit"
            op_margin <- "Financing Margin %" or "OPM %"
          (75 institutions use the first form, 481 the second; without this
          87% of the panel would carry a null revenue)
Output  : panel.csv, one row per (company, fiscal_year), 6,223 rows


B2. FEATURES  (score.py, build_features)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  equity            = Equity Capital + Reserves
  f_capital_buffer  = equity / total_assets
  f_capital_erosion = (equity[t] - equity[t-2]) / total_assets[t]
  f_leverage        = borrowings / equity              (equity > 0 only)
  f_roa             = net_profit / total_assets
  f_earnings_trend  = f_roa[t] - f_roa[t-3]
  f_interest_burden = interest_exp / revenue

  Also computed but UNWEIGHTED (retained so premortem.py can re-test them):
  f_cfo_ta          = cfo / total_assets
  f_borrow_cagr2    = (borrowings[t]/borrowings[t-2])^0.5 - 1

Lags are taken within company after sorting by fiscal year. Zero denominators
become null rather than infinity.


B3. PEER GROUPS  (score.py, peer_group)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  sector == "Banks"  ->  "Banks"                        (39-41 institutions)
  otherwise          ->  "NBFC-" + size tercile, by total_assets within year
                         -> Small | Mid | Large  (~167 each)

Rationale: ranking all 556 together put HDFC Bank at 66 against a sector median
of 50, because most of the "Finance" sector is small holding companies with no
borrowings. Narrowing the comparison moved HDFC Bank to 30-43.


B4. PERCENTILE TRANSFORM
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  orientation: negate capital_buffer, capital_erosion, roa, earnings_trend
               so that larger always means riskier

  s_<component> = rank(oriented, pct=True) * 100        within (fy, peer_group)

  nulls stay null and do not take a rank
  cells with fewer than MIN_PEERS = 20 institutions are left unscored

  DUAL ANCHOR = {capital_buffer, leverage}
    s = mean( rank within (fy, peer_group), rank within (fy) )
  Rationale: a peer group that is uniformly thin on capital would otherwise
  look safe by its own standards - exactly what separated the PCA banks from
  the private ones.


B5. EDGE CASES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  equity <= 0        -> s_capital_buffer and s_leverage forced to 100;
                        f_leverage left undefined rather than negative
  missing components -> weighted mean renormalised over those present
  coverage < 60%     -> row left unscored  (103 of 6,223 rows)


B6. AGGREGATION AND ALERT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  WEIGHTS   capital_buffer  0.30     leverage         0.12
            capital_erosion 0.20     interest_burden  0.10
            profitability   0.20     earnings_trend   0.08

  FDI = sum(s_c * w_c for available c) / sum(w_c for available c)

  bands     Low < 60 | Watch 60-80 | Elevated 80-90 | High >= 90

  fdi_base3 = median of the previous 3 fiscal years
              ( shift(1) then rolling(3, min_periods=2).median() )
  fdi_jump  = FDI - fdi_base3

  alert = (FDI >= 80) OR (FDI >= 60 AND fdi_jump >= +15)

The second leg exists because Yes Bank's FY2019 accounts scored 69 - below any
level threshold, but 29 points above its own three-year median of 39.9. It
fires 8.2 months before the moratorium and costs ~3 extra material names/year.


B7. POINT-IN-TIME DISCIPLINE  (validate.py)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  PUBLICATION_LAG_MONTHS = 3
  known_at(FY) = 31-Mar-FY + 3 months = 30-Jun-FY
  HORIZON_MONTHS = 24
  is_pre_event = 0 < (event_date - known_at) <= 24 months
  post_event   = event_date <= known_at   -> excluded from evaluation


B8. MATERIALITY FILTER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  is_material = (total_assets >= 500 cr) AND (revenue >= 25 cr)
  FY2023: 151 material of 552 scored.
Rationale: a third of the "Finance" sector is dormant shells with deeply
negative equity. The score correctly calls them insolvent and they swamp any
ranked screen, so they are flagged rather than dropped.


B9. MERTON MODULE  (merton.py)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Solve simultaneously for V and sigma_V:
      E  = V*N(d1) - D*exp(-r*T)*N(d2)
      sE = (V/E)*N(d1)*sigma_V
      d1 = [ln(V/D) + (r + sV^2/2)T] / (sV*sqrt(T));   d2 = d1 - sV*sqrt(T)
  Then:
      DD = [ln(V/D) + (r - sV^2/2)T] / (sV*sqrt(T));   PD = N(-DD)

  Method      fixed-point iteration from V = E+D, sV = sE*E/(E+D)
              MAX_ITER 300, TOL 1e-6; non-convergence returns null
  Parameters  T = 1 year; r = 6.5%; default point = 1.0 x borrowings
              volatility window 250 trading days, min 120 observations
              annualisation sqrt(252); solver evaluated on a weekly grid

  MARKET CAP vs VOLATILITY use different price series:
    volatility  adjusted for every corporate action (splits, bonuses, rights)
    market cap  raw close x shares, where
                face_value[t] = face_value_current * product of SPLIT factors
                                after t                   (bonuses EXCLUDED)
                shares[t]     = equity_capital[t] / face_value[t]
  Bonuses are excluded from that product because a bonus capitalises reserves,
  so equity capital already rises with the share count; counting it twice would
  halve the pre-event market cap.


B10. CORPORATE ACTIONS  (resolve_splits.py)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Detection: single-day ratio close/prevclose within 1.5% of 1/k or k for
             k in [1.5, 2, 2.5, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 10, 20], move >= 18%

  Guards, each added after a real false positive:
    1.25 excluded      0.80 is exactly India's 20% circuit limit; including it
                       labelled three of Reliance Capital's 2019 crash days as
                       bonus issues
    market breadth     reject days where >6% of symbols moved >10%; genuine
                       split days show 1.4-3.4% breadth, 19 Mar 2020 showed
                       12.5% and was reading a COVID crash as a 1.5x split
    minimum history    60 prior trading days (caught Ujjivan's listing pop)
    no clustering      drop pairs within 30 days (illiquid-quote artefact)

  Classification: split if equity capital is unchanged across the action,
                  bonus if equity capital jumped by the same factor
  Result: 12 actions, all verifiable against the public record.

  NOTE: an earlier version assumed NSE's PREVCLOSE was corporate-action
  adjusted. IT IS NOT. On Yes Bank's split day the file reports CLOSE 377.05
  against PREVCLOSE 1880.10 - the raw drop.

C1. DATA  (fdic_fetch.py)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Endpoints   https://api.fdic.gov/banks/failures     (4,117 records, 1934-)
              https://api.fdic.gov/banks/financials   (quarterly call reports)
  Join key    CERT  (+ REPDTE for financials)
  Fields      ASSET, EQ, DEP, NETINC, ROA, INTINC, EINTEXP, NPERFV, LNATRES
  Window      2003Q1 - 2023Q4, 84 quarters
  Result      588,177 bank-quarters, 10,459 institutions, 563 matched failures
  Transport   curl via subprocess - urllib hangs against this endpoint
  No authentication required. One request per quarter, limit 10,000.


C2. COMPONENTS  (fdic_model.py)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  CAPITAL        capital_buffer      EQ / ASSET                        0.20
                 capital_erosion     8-quarter change in EQ / ASSET    0.14
                 leverage            (ASSET - EQ) / EQ                 0.08
  ASSET QUALITY  npa_ratio           NPERFV                            0.18
                 reserve_shortfall   NPERFV - LNATRES/ASSET*100        0.08
  EARNINGS       profitability       ROA                               0.14
                 earnings_trend      12-quarter change in ROA          0.06
  FUNDING        funding_contraction -(DEP[t]/DEP[t-4] - 1)            0.06
                 interest_burden     EINTEXP / INTINC                  0.06

  Peer group    size tercile by ASSET within quarter
  Filing lag    45 days after quarter end
  Baseline      median of previous 12 quarters, shifted 1


C3. DEVELOPMENT / HELD-OUT SPLIT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  development   failures before 2013-01-01   (529, the 2008-12 credit wave)
  held-out      failures 2013-01-01 onward   (63 evaluable to 2023-12-31)
  These are different REGIMES, not merely different dates.

  Component selection: keep if development AUC >= 0.5. All nine clear it.
    npa_ratio 0.875, reserve_shortfall 0.856, profitability 0.830,
    leverage 0.815, capital_buffer 0.814, earnings_trend 0.783,
    interest_burden 0.779, capital_erosion 0.768, funding_contraction 0.580

  Threshold selection rule, FIXED BEFORE SEARCHING:
    maximise development recall SUBJECT TO watchlist <= 5% of banks
    -> alert = (FDI >= 85) OR (FDI >= 65 AND jump >= +20)

  An earlier unconstrained rule (maximise recall, tie-break on watchlist)
  selected an 18%-of-system watchlist. The capacity of the person reading the
  list belongs in the objective, not in the commentary afterwards.

PART D - KNOWN LIMITATIONS
================================================================================

D1. STATISTICAL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- India: 18 events, 13 of them PCA placements. Recall CI 52-94%.
- US held-out: 63 failures, 2014-2022 contributing fewer than 35.
- US development is a single crisis with a common cause.
- Component selection used the same events it was tested on (India); the
  held-out test mitigates but does not remove this.


D2. IT DOES NOT BEAT A ONE-LINE BENCHMARK            [MATERIAL]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Tested on the US held-out period at a matched 2.8% watchlist rate:

  signal                      AUC     caught    precision
  nine-component index       0.894     56/63       3.98%
  Texas Ratio                0.869     56/63       4.02%
  equity / assets alone      0.913     56/63       3.57%

All three catch THE SAME 56 of 63 failures. The composite adds no recall over
a single ratio any analyst can compute in one line, and equity/assets alone
has the higher AUC on the held-out period.

This also corrects an earlier claim in this project. On the FULL US period the
composite does beat leverage (0.861 vs 0.837), and that was reported as
settling the "AUC trap" argument. On the HELD-OUT period the ranking reverses.
The honest statement is that the composite's advantage is not robust to the
period chosen.

What the composite does buy is interpretability - a decomposable score with an
explicit reason per alert - not accuracy.


D3. REGIME BLINDNESS                                 [MATERIAL]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
US held-out recall, by count and by assets:

  2013-2022    53 of 58 (91%)      $20.1bn of $20.4bn  (99%)
  2023          1 of 5             $0.1bn of $532.2bn  (0.02%)
  overall      54 of 63 (86%)                          (4%)

The three misses are Silicon Valley Bank, Signature Bank and First Republic.

SVB's last call report (31 Dec 2022, public before the 10 Mar 2023 failure):
  equity/assets 7.39%   ROA 0.96%   non-performing 0.066%
  deposits 175,378,000 against 191,431,000 a year earlier  (-8.4%)
  index 59.0 against an 85 threshold

One component fired: deposit contraction, 94th percentile for SVB and 98th for
Signature. It was the weakest component in development (AUC 0.580) and carries
6% weight, so it could not move the composite.

Cause: the 2008-12 wave was credit-driven and every stage is visible in these
ratios. 2023 was duration-driven - unrealised losses on held-to-maturity
securities, not recognised in reported figures, plus a concentrated uninsured
deposit base. None of that is in the feature set.

The fix is specified but UNTESTED, deliberately: adding fields after seeing
which failures were missed, then reporting improved recall on those same
failures, would be fitting to the test set. Required fields, all available in
call reports and not pulled here:
    - accumulated other comprehensive income / unrealised securities losses
    - held-to-maturity share of assets
    - uninsured deposit share
    - securities duration


D4. INTERPRETATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- The index is a RANK, not a probability. Precision is 3.93%: most institutions
  above the threshold do not fail.
- A long-standing alert is not a warning. A five-year-old alert describes a
  weak balance sheet; it anticipates nothing.
- Merton implied PD is a model output under strong assumptions (lognormal
  assets, single maturity, no external support). Its movement is informative;
  its level is not.
- No market read means NO SECOND OPINION, not safety. 409 of 562 Indian
  institutions have no Merton estimate.


D5. DATA
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- The Indian archive is a third-party scrape, not reconciled against primary
  filings.
- Four Indian failures remain unevaluable: DHFL, IL&FS, PMC Bank, Altico.
- Indian accounting data is annual; the index moves once per filing cycle.
- Call-report figures are as filed; the 2017 US miss (Washington Federal Bank
  for Savings) was a fraud case in which the filings were false.
- Size terciles are recomputed each period, so an institution can change peer
  group - and score - without changing itself.
